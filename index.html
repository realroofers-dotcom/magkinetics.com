Turning a negative into a positive

Traffic has only ever taken energy. MagKinetics gives some back.

Until now our use of cars and trucks has counted only as a cost in the energy equation. MagKinetics was founded to change that: to produce clean electricity from normal driving, without any change to the vehicle, our mobility or our freedom to drive.

How it works

A generator in the road, turned by the cars above it

  1. Generator units sit beneath the road. Each holds a rotor with magnets around its rim and a stator with copper windings.
  2. A passing vehicle pulls on the magnets. The steel of a car or truck attracts the magnets and urges the rotor to turn.
  3. The turning rotor makes electricity. The moving magnetic field induces a current in the windings, the induction Michael Faraday discovered.
  4. The power goes to the grid. An inverter feeds the current into the utility power grid.

The units belong where vehicles already slow down and energy is normally lost to braking: off-ramps, downhill slopes and approaches to traffic signals. The road surface stays smooth.

Questions people asked us

Is it a perpetual motion machine?

No. The system obeys the conservation of energy and the laws of thermodynamics. It captures energy from vehicles at points where that energy would otherwise be lost to braking.

Does it make cars use more gas?

At a slowing-down point, such as a signal or an exit ramp, the magnetic drag helps the vehicle slow down, adding braking force. It is placed where drivers are already braking.

Is the magnetic field harmful?

Inside a car, the body of the car acts as a shield against the field.

How much energy can it produce?

In 2008 the company set a goal of 2 megawatts in a quarter mile of road. That figure was a goal, not a measured result; measuring it is the job of the prototype.

History

Ten years on one idea

  • March 2004 · BostonAt a sustainable energy conference, listening to wind, solar and biofuel advocates, Mark Nejmeh asked what everyone in the room had in common. Every one of them had come over a road. The idea was born.
  • After 2004A college friend with a background in economics formed the corporation with him. Engineers and physicists joined.
  • January 9, 2008U.S. patent application No. 11/971,653 filed: "System and Method for Generating Electricity from Automobile Traffic."
  • April 15, 2008 · New YorkMagKinetics announced that its president, Mark Nejmeh, would travel to Dubai, U.A.E., to present the patent-pending technology to investors.
  • 2008A laboratory in Neptune, New Jersey, and a prototype team of engineers from Purdue University, MIT and the University of Houston.
  • January 8, 2009International application PCT/US2009/030423 filed.
  • July 16, 2009Published worldwide as WO 2009/089336 A1.
  • TodayMark Nejmeh still considers the invention viable. He intends to build a prototype and to apply for an updated patent.
